# Most Valuable Parent: the brief on one page

*October 7, 2026 · Owner Greg Harris · Sign-off Heather Hannan-Kramer · The full brief holds the detail.*

## What it is

Most Valuable Parent (MVP) is a planning app and support system for busy parents. A parent gives MVP whatever arrives in her week, by sharing a message from any app, forwarding an email, photographing a handout or simply talking, and MVP turns it into one plan for the day and the week. She also connects the calendar she already keeps, so what is already there reaches the plan without her sending it. The plan shows who goes where, who takes them, what goes with each child, and what still needs her.

The plan is what MVP delivers, and around it are the people and the help she relies on. Her inner circle is the people she trusts with her children, such as her partner, her family and the family's doctors and tutors. Her village is the other parents and families who share her children's week, and she talks with them in private groups. MVP's coach is one more helper beside them. It sorts what she sends, keeps what applies to her children, drafts the requests she sends to other people, and answers the parenting questions she would otherwise take to Reddit.

## The problem

In most families one parent, usually the mother, carries the planning for the whole household. School emails, newsletters, group chats, texts and paper handouts all land on her, most of it covers other people's children, and none of it is connected. The hard part is not knowing what is happening. It is working out who drives, when everyone leaves, what each child takes and who to ask, and then doing it all again when someone's day changes.

## Who it is for

We design and market for mothers first, specifically the mother who holds the week in her head. She lives on her phone, talks to it more than she types, and has no interest in copying her life into another app. The product still works for any household, because it asks who is in the family rather than assigning roles.

## What makes it different

Every new app will have an AI assistant, so the assistant is not what sets MVP apart, and four other things are. MVP starts from what she already receives and what is already on her calendar, so she never retypes anything, and when she adds something to her calendar that runs into the plan, MVP tells her. The plan is honest about where things stand, because every item shows who owns it and whether they have said yes, and nothing is ever shown as finished. When someone's day changes, that part of the plan reopens and she sees her options. Finally, MVP connects her to her people while leaving her in charge, because she sends every message herself and people see only what she sends them.

## How it works

She connects the calendar she already keeps, whether Google Calendar, Apple Calendar or Outlook, right after she creates her account, and MVP brings in what is already there, such as a weekly therapy appointment or ballet on Thursdays. From then on, when she adds something to her calendar, it appears in the plan, and if it runs into something already planned, MVP points out the clash and offers ways to fix it. Today reads like a departures board, with the next departure, what goes with each child, a row for each person through the day, and one card holding the decision that most needs her, and items from her calendar carry a small calendar mark so she can tell them apart. Game plan holds the whole week, and she can move, assign or annotate anything without help. Team holds her inner circle, her village and her village groups. Kids holds a page for each child and a card for each school, kept current from what she sends in. The coach sits in its own tab for anything she wants to talk through.

## How it should feel

She should feel calm and in control, and never watched, rushed or judged. MVP never tells her the week is finished, because a parent's week never is. The coach is warm and direct and names every child. The product must feel made by people, because a parent will not trust her family's schedule to something that reads as generated. The visual style, Hearth, is closer to a well-made paper planner than to a dashboard.

## What MVP will and won't do

MVP never sends anything to another person on her behalf. The coach drafts each message in her voice, and she reads it, changes what she wants and sends it herself. MVP reads her calendar but never adds to it or changes it, so her calendar stays exactly as she keeps it. The coach never reads the conversations in her village groups, and the people she sends requests to see only those requests, never her plan or her conversations with the coach. The coach also stays within its limits, so it doesn't find providers, buy anything or give medical, legal or custody advice, and when a question needs a doctor, a therapist or a teacher, it names the person she already has. MVP isn't a content feed or a directory either, and its staff-written articles appear only when something in her week makes one useful.
