# MVP vocabulary guide

*For Heather Hannan-Kramer's review of on-screen wording · Drawn from the story fixtures, version 3.25, October 20, 2026*

This guide collects the words MVP uses again and again across the app, such as the names of the tabs, the labels on a plan item and the way the coach introduces itself on a card. Reviewing these first lets us agree on changes that apply everywhere before anyone works through the screens one by one, because a change to a repeated word should be made once rather than rediscovered on every screen. Every example below is quoted from the story fixtures, which remain the source of truth for every word on screen. If this guide and the fixtures ever disagree, the fixtures win, and the disagreement should be raised so this guide can be corrected.

A few conventions help in reading the tables. A middle dot (·) separates pieces of text that sit side by side or one after another on the same screen. Square brackets, as in "[Mallory]", stand for a child's name chip, which is drawn as a filled pill rather than typed out. Words in quotation marks appear exactly as written, and examples use Charlotte and Edward's household, because version 3.25 covers that family only.

## What's fixed

Some things are not up for change in this review, because they come from product decisions rather than word choices. You can still propose a change to any of them, but it will be recorded as a decision for Greg and you to make together, with its effect on the rest of the product worked through, rather than handled as a word edit.

**The product name.** The product is MVP, and in the wordmark "MVP" never travels alone, so it always appears with "Most Valuable Parent" as a unit. The splash screen shows the MVP mark with "Most Valuable Parent" beneath it. "Lombardi" is the internal code name for this build, and it never appears in the product or in investor materials.

**The coach's voice.** The coach is warm and direct, writes in the present tense, speaks to the parent and refers to children by name. It never opens with words like "Okay" or "Alright", never praises the parent and never describes what it has just done. It never uses em dashes, sentence fragments, sets of three parallel phrases or planner jargon such as window, slot, block, buffer or lane, and it never uses a pronoun in place of a child's name. Labels, headings and buttons are interface text rather than coach speech, so they follow plain language instead of these rules, although they share the ban on fragments in anything that tells her something.

**The locked product decisions.** Wording must never contradict three rules. Nothing in a plan is ever shown as settled, so no screen calls a plan finished in any form. Nothing goes to another person unless the parent sends it herself, which is why drafts are written in her voice and every send button is hers to press. The coach does not read her chats or her groups, and people see only what she sends them. The product keeps these rules through how it behaves, so no line on screen repeats them as a disclaimer, except where a line tells someone something they need to know, such as the Privacy card in Settings and the line at the top of a chat.

## The six rules from October 20

Greg set six vocabulary rules on October 20, 2026, and every screen in version 3.25 follows them. They apply to everything MVP writes, including the drafts it writes in the parent's voice. Words that a person types or says, such as the parent's own notes, other parents' posts and texts, and quoted school documents, keep that person's own phrasing.

**1. Every time says am or pm, with no space.** Both ends of a range carry it, so a parent who reads only the second time never has to look back, and noon is written "12:00pm". Durations such as "Drive 10 min" are not times and carry no qualifier. For example, a range reads "3:00pm to 3:45pm".

**2. Every day says which week it is in.** The screen says "today", "tomorrow" or "yesterday" when one of them applies, "this" and the day for any other day in the same Monday to Sunday week, and "last" and the day for the week before. A day in the following week says "next", the day and the date, because "next Friday" is the phrase parents most often read two ways. For example, "next Tuesday, Oct. 20". Today's dateline, the dates on the onboarding draft, the week strip's letters, the short day labels on a person page and the game plan's day headings are exempt, because a heading already names the week.

**3. Places use their full names.** Someone who has never heard of a place should be able to tell what kind of place it is, and a long name wraps to a second line rather than being shortened. Only "Home", a family's home such as "the Okafors'", and the names a parent gives her own groups are exempt. For example, the app says "Brightwater Family Therapy" and "Call Northside Pediatrics", never a short form.

**4. Where no chip names the child, the words do.** A row led by a name chip keeps the chip in place of the child's name, so the row never repeats it. Everywhere else, including headlines, the title on Now's pass, the coach's lines, notifications and drafts, the child is named the way a parent would say it aloud. For example, Later today reads "[Mallory] Pick up at Westview Kindergarten", while the pass reads "Pick Mallory up at Westview Kindergarten".

**5. Anything about leaving names where she leaves from.** The one exception is the status pill, which keeps its short form, "Time to leave", because the route above it already names the place. For example, "Leave Westview Kindergarten at 2:30pm" or "Remind me to leave home".

**6. Her circle and her people.** The people she trusts with her children and her private life are her circle, everyone else who shares her children's week are her people, and a group she creates inside MVP is a group. The Team tab keeps its name. For example, the Team tab's sections read "Chats · Groups · Circle · People".

## Words we avoid

The fixtures name several words and forms that never appear on screen. Each one is avoided for a reason tied to the product's rules, so replacing it with a close synonym usually breaks the same rule.

| Word or form | Why it is avoided |
| ----- | ----- |
| Set, covered, sorted, done or final, said of a plan | Nothing in a plan is ever shown as settled, and neither the coach nor any other line on screen calls a plan any of these. The counts "11 done" and "1 done" describe ticked items and not the plan. |
| Inner circle | Retired on October 20, 2026. The trusted group is her circle. |
| Village, and village group | Retired on October 20, 2026. Everyone else is her people, and a group she creates is a group. |
| Your people, as a tab name | Versions 3.1 and 3.2 used it for the tab now called Team. |
| Going with list | The earlier name for the children's part of the checklist. A child page keeps its "Going with, by day" section. |
| Uppercase labels and letter-spaced eyebrows | The type rules allow neither, and bold words inside paragraphs are also avoided. |
| Send, on the capture bar | Send on its own means sending something to another person, so the capture bar's arrow is labeled "Give this to the coach". |
| A weekday named alone | Every day says which week it is in, so "Friday" becomes "this Friday", "last Friday" or "next Friday, Oct. 23". |
| Shortened place names | Places use their full names, and a long name wraps rather than being cut. |
| A child's name repeated after a chip | The chip already names the child, so a row never reads "[Mallory] Collect Mallory at Westview Kindergarten". |
| A pronoun for a child in the coach's lines | The coach always uses the child's name, which is why an earlier line that said "collect her" was rewritten. |
| Praise and celebration | The coach never praises the parent, so a finished checklist folds away with a check and no coach line, sound or confetti. |
| Em dashes and fragments | Neither appears in the coach's lines or in any line that tells her something. |
